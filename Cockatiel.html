What is a Cockatiel?

Cockatiels are small parrots native to Australia. They are known for their expressive crests, bright orange cheek patches, and friendly personalities. Because of their gentle nature and relatively small size, they are one of the most popular pet birds in the world.

In the wild, cockatiels live in flocks and travel long distances in search of food and water. As pets, they can form strong bonds with their owners and enjoy spending time outside the cage, exploring and interacting.

Appearance

Cockatiels typically have a slender body, long tail, and a distinctive crest of feathers on top of their head. The wild-type color is mostly grey with a yellow face and orange cheeks, but many color mutations exist, such as lutino (yellow/white), pied, pearl, and cinnamon.

  • Crest: Shows their mood—raised when curious or excited, flattened when scared or angry.
  • Size: Usually around 30–33 cm from head to tail.
  • Lifespan: With good care, they can live 15–20 years or more.

Personality and Behavior

Cockatiels are social, playful, and often quite vocal. They may whistle, mimic simple sounds, and enjoy interacting with people. Many cockatiels like head scratches and will gently lower their head to ask for attention.

They can be shy at first, but with patience and gentle handling, they usually become more confident. Providing toys, perches, and time outside the cage helps keep them mentally and physically active.

Basic Care

Taking care of a cockatiel means providing a safe environment, a healthy diet, and daily interaction. They should not be kept alone in a cage without attention for long periods, as they are flock animals and need companionship.

  • Cage: Spacious, with horizontal bars for climbing and multiple perches.
  • Diet: A mix of high-quality pellets, seeds in moderation, and fresh vegetables.
  • Water: Fresh, clean water available at all times.
  • Enrichment: Toys, swings, and safe branches to chew on.
  • Health: Regular vet checkups and observation for changes in behavior or droppings.
